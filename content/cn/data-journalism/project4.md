description: |
  As China’s population ages and population mobility increases, the demand for medical care away from one’s home city has also grown. Patient companions have therefore emerged as a new profession. Their rise reflects several broader challenges in China, including limited healthcare resources, an aging population, and increasing social isolation.

  This project draws on public industry data, data from China’s National Health Commission, job postings on Chinese recruitment websites, and the experiences of frontline workers and users. It explores the social factors behind the rise of patient companions, uses data to describe the profession, and discusses the lack of industry regulation and the broader social issues behind it.

  This project was completed as the final assignment for a data journalism course.
