\resumeProject
  {Should I Stay Calm: How Does the Combination of Visual and Textual Frames Influence Expressions of Anger in Short Video Comment Sections?}
  {Zihao Li.*}
  {AEJMC 2026}
  {{}[\href{https://github.com/MiShenng/visual_textual_frames_anger}{\textcolor{darkblue}{\faGlobe}}]}
\resumeProjectItemListStart
  \item Developed an AI-agent-based approach to analyze visual and textual affective frames in short videos, using machine learning to measure anger expressed in audience comments and examine how different multimodal frame combinations relate to anger during public controversies in China.
  \item Found that visual and textual frames tended to align and were independently associated with audience anger. A two-way ANOVA showed significant main effects for both modalities but no significant interaction.

  {}
\resumeProjectItemListStart

\resumeProject
  {Moral Cues in Visual Misinformation: Visual Event Schemas and the Moral Reorientation of Authentic Images}
  {Xinxin Li, \& Zihao Li.*}
  {Manuscript in Preparation}
  {{}[\href{https://github.com/MiShenng/moral-images}{\textcolor{darkblue}{\faGlobe}}]}
\resumeProjectItemListStart
  \item Combined Moral Foundations Theory with visual event schemas to examine how false captions reconstruct the moral meanings of authentic images in cheap fakes, providing a framework for understanding image--text interactions in multimodal misinformation.
  \item Contributed to research design, data collection, analysis, and manuscript writing. Paired false claims with the original contexts of corresponding images, extracted variables using computer vision, and conducted latent class analysis and linear regression.
\resumeProjectItemListEnd

\resumeProject
  {From Emotion to Hostility: Discrete Negative Emotions and Gender-Directed Incivility in Chinese Gender-Issue Short Videos}
  {Zihao Li.*}
  {Under Review, ICA 2027}
  {{}[\href{https://github.com/MiShenng/Gender-Polarization-and-Hate-Speech}{\textcolor{darkblue}{\faGlobe}}]}
\resumeProjectItemListStart
  \item Designed and conducted a computational study of gender-related discussions on Chinese TikTok. Used a multimodal emotion model to measure discrete emotions in videos and developed a supervised classifier to identify incivility and gender-directed hostility in comments. Applied binomial regression and interaction tests to examine emotion--incivility associations.
  \item Found that anger was positively associated with incivility, while fear showed a negative association. These associations varied with video content context and the targets of hostile comments.
\resumeProjectItemListEnd

\resumeProject
  {See It, Click It? How Visual Clickbait Shapes Stratified User Engagement in Health-Related Videos on China's Bilibili}
  {Zihao Li.*}
  {Under Review, ICA 2027}
  {{}[\href{https://github.com/MiShenng/Bilibili-Health-Visual-Clickbait}{\textcolor{darkblue}{\faGlobe}}]}
\resumeProjectItemListStart
  \item Examined visual clickbait in health-related Bilibili videos using computational methods. Collected thumbnails and engagement data, using LLM-assisted coding and Chinese-CLIP to measure visual cues and thumbnail--title congruence. Applied negative binomial regression to examine engagement outcomes.
  \item Found that binary clickbait measures may obscure differences among visual strategies. Clickbait was positively associated with views; after adjusting for view counts, it was negatively associated with likes, coins, favorites, and shares, highlighting differences between attention and deeper engagement.
\resumeProjectItemListEnd
